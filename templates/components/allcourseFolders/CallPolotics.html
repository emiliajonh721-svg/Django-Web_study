20 words

Democracy

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A system of government where citizens hold power and elect their representatives.

Example:

In a democracy, citizens have the right to choose their leaders through regular elections.

Constitution

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

The fundamental set of laws and principles that govern a country.

Example:

The constitution protects the basic rights and freedoms of all citizens.

Legislation

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A law or set of laws suggested by a government and made official by a parliament.

Example:

The parliament passed new legislation to improve public education.

Election

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A formal process in which people vote to choose a person for public office.

Example:

Voter turnout was very high during last month's national election.

Ballot

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A piece of paper or electronic form used to record a person's vote.

Example:

Citizens cast their ballot in private at the local polling station.

Candidate

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A person who applies for a job or is nominated for election to a political office.

Example:

Each candidate presented their plan for the economy during the debate.

Campaign

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

An organized series of actions and speeches to win an election.

Example:

The political campaign lasted for three months before voting day.

Policy

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A course of action or set of rules adopted by a government or political party.

Example:

The ministry announced a new policy to support renewable energy.

Parliament

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

The group of elected politicians who make and change the laws of a country.

Example:

Members of parliament debated the national budget for several hours.

Diplomacy

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

The profession or skill of managing international relations between countries.

Example:

The two nations resolved their border dispute through peaceful diplomacy.

Sovereignty

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

The supreme power or authority of a state to govern itself without outside interference.

Example:

Every independent nation seeks to protect its territorial sovereignty.

Coalition

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A temporary alliance of two or more political parties to form a government.

Example:

Because no single party won a majority, they formed a coalition government.

Ratify

Part of speech: Verb

Meaning

To sign or give formal consent to a treaty, contract, or agreement, making it officially valid.

Example:

The senate voted to ratify the international trade agreement.

Veto

Part of speech: Noun / Verb

Meaning

The constitutional right to reject a decision or proposal made by a law-making body.

Example:

The president used a veto to block the proposed tax bill.

Amendment

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A formal change or addition made to a law or constitution.

Example:

Lawmakers proposed an amendment to update the existing environmental law.

Referendum

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A general vote by the electorate on a single political question.

Example:

The government held a national referendum to decide on the constitutional change.

Bureaucracy

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A system of government in which most important decisions are made by state officials rather than elected representatives.

Example:

Many business owners complained that excessive bureaucracy slowed down the permit process.

Ideology

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A system of ideas and ideals that forms the basis of economic or political theory and policy.

Example:

Voters often support parties whose political ideology matches their own values.

Bipartisan

Part of speech: Adjective

Meaning

Involving the agreement or cooperation of two political parties that usually oppose each other.

Example:

The infrastructure bill received strong bipartisan support from both major parties.

Constituent

Part of speech: Noun

Meaning

A person who lives and votes in a specific area represented by an elected official.

Example:

The representative held a town hall meeting to listen to the concerns of every constituent.
